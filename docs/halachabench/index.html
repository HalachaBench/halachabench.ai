The semicha exam leaderboard

30 AI models answered the 60 questions of five Chief Rabbinate semicha papers from the Cheshvan 5787 sitting: three times each, in Hebrew, closed-book. Claude Fable 5.2 leads with 85.8.

Papers

Judge

Provisional. One AI judge, GPT-6 Astra, which is itself on the board, has graded every answer. The rubrics were written and reviewed by AI models; no rabbi has reviewed them or graded an answer yet. These are not exam grades, and a high score is not authority to pasken.

score 95% interval worst of 3 attempts

Score: how much of the rubric an answer achieves, 0 to 100, averaged over 60 questions and 3 attempts. Worst of 3: each question's lowest-scoring attempt, averaged. The 95% intervals come from a bootstrap over questions (10,000 replicates) and cover only the choice of questions, not judge or rubric error. Select a column heading to sort.

Which models are clearly apart?

Overlapping intervals do not by themselves mean two models are tied, so models were also compared question by question.

  • Claude Fable 5.2 and Gemini 4 Carbon lead: they are not clearly apart (Fable 5.2 ahead by 3.5 points, 95% interval −0.3 to 7.1). Fable 5.2 is clearly ahead of every other model: of Claude Fable 5.1 by 6.0 points (3.0 to 9.1), of Gemini 4 Argon by 7.5 (3.7 to 11.2) and of GPT-5.6 Sol by 8.5 (4.2 to 13.4). Gemini 4 Carbon is clearly ahead of Gemini 4 Argon (4.1, 1.8 to 6.4), GPT-6 Astra (9.0, 4.1 to 13.7) and every model below them, but not clearly apart from Claude Fable 5.1 (2.6 behind Carbon, −0.9 to 6.1) or GPT-5.6 Sol (5.0, −0.4 to 10.8).
  • Not clearly apart: Claude Fable 5.1, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol (Fable ahead of Argon by 1.5 points, 95% interval −1.8 to 4.9, and of GPT-5.6 Sol by 2.5, −2.5 to 7.9); GPT-5.6 Sol with GPT-6 Astra (3.9, −0.9 to 8.5) and GPT-6.1 Sol (4.5, −0.5 to 8.9); Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-6 Astra (4.9, −0.3 to 10.0); GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5; Gemini 3.8 Flash and Muse Wonder Full (3.6, −1.2 to 8.1); GPT-6 Sol, Muse Wonder and Claude Opus 4.8 (GPT-6 Sol ahead of Opus 4.8 by 0.6, −4.6 to 5.4, and Muse Wonder within 0.4 of both); Muse Spark 1.3 with GPT-6.1 Terra (1.6, −3.3 to 6.8) and GPT-6 Luna (3.9, −0.6 to 8.3); GPT-6 Luna, Grok 4.7, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 5.5; GPT-6.1 Terra and GPT-6 Luna; Grok 4.3, Muse Glimmer 30B and Claude Haiku 5.5, and Claude Haiku 5.5 with Qwen3.8 27B; most pairs among ranks 22 to 27.
  • Clearly apart: Claude Fable 5.1 is ahead of every model below it except Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol (by 6.4 points over GPT-6 Astra, 1.6 to 11.1); GPT-5.6 Sol is ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 (8.2, 2.0 to 14.1) and every model below it; Gemini 4 Argon is ahead of GPT-6.1 Sol (5.5, 0.8 to 10.2) and every model below it; GPT-6.1 Terra is ahead of Grok 4.7 (4.7, 0.5 to 9.1), Claude Sonnet 4.6 (5.0, 0.1 to 10.0) and Claude Sonnet 5.5 (5.9, 0.8 to 10.9); Claude Opus 5.5 is ahead of Muse Wonder Full (8.3, 4.5 to 12.0), and Muse Wonder Full is ahead of GPT-6 Sol (12.1, 7.8 to 16.6) and every model below it; Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-6 Sol and Muse Wonder are apart from every other model but one another; Muse Spark 1.3 is ahead of Grok 4.7 (6.4, 2.2 to 10.7) and every model below it; Muse Ember is below Claude Sonnet 5.5 (13.4, 9.2 to 17.7) and ahead of Grok 4.3 (4.6, 1.0 to 8.2), Muse Glimmer 30B (4.8, 2.2 to 7.6) and Claude Haiku 5.5 (6.7, 3.4 to 10.2); gpt-oss-20b is below every other model.
  • Across generations: Claude Opus 5.5 is 21.0 points above Claude Opus 4.8 (17.4 to 24.9); Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 are not clearly apart (0.9, −3.0 to 4.9). In the other direction, GPT-5.6 Sol is 28.6 points above the newer GPT-6 Sol (24.2 to 33.2), and ahead of it on 57 of the 59 questions both answered. GPT-6 Sol did not make more errors; it wrote shorter answers (a median of about 200 words against about 310, with less thinking) that covered fewer of the sources and rulings the rubrics ask for (48% of the requirements, against 77%).
  • Within the Muse models: Muse Wonder Full is 12.5 points above Muse Wonder (9.3 to 15.8), and Muse Wonder 12.0 above Muse Spark 1.3 (7.5 to 16.6). Muse Ember, released on 9 November as Meta's lightest Muse model, is 20.9 below Muse Spark 1.3 (16.7 to 25.3) and 4.8 above Muse Glimmer 30B, the open-weight Muse model run locally, which is 25.7 below Muse Spark 1.3 (21.7 to 30.0).

Notes on individual runs

  1. ¹Some runs produced no answer text. The model used up the output limit we set while still thinking (DictaLM 9 of 180 and Qwen3.8 27B 9 of 180; the limits were 57,088 and 65,536 tokens, the latter below what Qwen's model card allows), or stopped after thinking without writing an answer (Qwen3.8 27B 10 of 180). Each such run was tried once more; those still without an answer score 0, as every failed answer does.
  2. ²Llama 4 Scout failed one pre-run check: asked whether it had any tools, it claimed a web-search tool it did not have. It ran in a sealed container with no network and no tools, so it was kept, and the failure is disclosed here.
  3. ³Gemini 4 Argon's run stopped when the account's usage quota ran out, and finished on purchased Google AI credits (a recorded amendment; the credits change billing only). During the run, a manual check of the Antigravity program triggered its auto-update, so 84 of Argon's 180 answers (on 28 questions) ran on version 1.4.12 instead of the registered 1.4.10. On those 28 questions, Argon's gap to the four models nearest it is within 2.6 points of its gap on the other 32 (permutation p 0.57 to 0.82), so the version made no visible difference. The answers were kept and are disclosed here.
  4. ⁴Grok 4.7 and Grok 4.3 ran through xAI's Responses API: no tools in the request, responses not stored, reasoning effort high, xAI's recommended sampling (temperature 0.7, top_p 0.95). Every response was checked: none used a tool or a source, and none was cut off. See Checks on the Grok results.
  5. ⁵GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Sol ran on Codex like the other GPT models, with the same settings. GPT-5.6 Sol's Codex exposes a different set of tool definitions, which is recorded. On all three attempts at one question (5787-cheshvan-teluyot-yishum-q11), for both models, OpenAI's safety system flagged the answer partway through and signalled a switch to a smaller model. The harness stops any run that receives an event it does not recognise, so that no answer by another model is recorded under this one's name; those three answers per model score 0, as every failed answer does. These two runs used a later version of the runner code; the only changes are the pre-run check terms and an option to rerun a single attempt, and the code that sends the question and records the answer is unchanged. GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 are not offered on the Codex sign-in used here.
  6. ⁶Muse Wonder Full, Muse Wonder, Muse Spark 1.3 and Muse Ember ran through Meta's Muse Code CLI on a Muse Code subscription: a pinned program version (1.4.14), effort high, a fresh process with its own home folder for each answer, the empty preset, and the web, shell and file-writing tools switched off. Every run was monitored live, and any action other than writing the answer would have disqualified it; none was. All 180 answers of each model were completed. Muse Ember was added the day Meta released it. Muse Code keeps its tool definitions and built-in instructions in the model's context. Some Muse answers (about 25 of 540) format their citations as Markdown links to placeholder file paths or web addresses, as a coding assistant would; the citations themselves are ordinary, and the answers were graded as written.
  7. ⁷Claude Fable 5.2 ran like the other Claude models (Claude Code CLI, every tool switched off, effort high), but on Claude Code 2.1.290 instead of 2.1.283: Anthropic's API refuses this model on the older version. Like every Claude model since 2 November, it ran on the project's own Claude account, whose email address, which Claude Code shows the model, contains the project's name.
  8. ⁸Gemini 4 Carbon ran like Gemini 4 Argon (Antigravity CLI 1.4.10, effort high, every run monitored live; none used a tool). It thinks far longer than any other model: a median of about 69,000 output tokens and 13 minutes per answer, against about 14,000 for Argon. Two attempts at one question timed out after 30 minutes with no reply, twice each, and were run once more under the registered retry rule; both then answered, so all 180 answers are scored. During its grading, OpenAI's service began sending a new kind of status message with every GPT-6 Astra call, one that only repeats the requested reasoning effort. The harness stops any call that receives a message it does not recognise, so grading halted. A recorded amendment lets the harness accept that one message when it exactly repeats the requested effort (any other message still stops the call); this changes the judge protocol's hash, and the new hash is registered as equivalent to the old one. The grading begun under the old hash was set aside, and all of Carbon's answers were graded again from the start. The other 29 models' grades are unchanged.
