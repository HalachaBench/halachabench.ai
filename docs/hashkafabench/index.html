Is your AI stringent or lenient?

Religious Jews already ask AI models everyday halachic questions. HashkafaBench measures which way each model leans, machmir or maikel, in seven areas of halacha.

Questions
288 everyday questions, asked in English as an Ashkenazi Jew
Judge
Claude Opus 5.5; GPT-6 Astra re-graded 15% (91% exact agreement)
Data
Results of 6 November 2026, on GitHub

Descriptive, not psak. These results describe how models answer. Rubric positions were written and source-checked by the team and AI models, not yet reviewed by rabbis, and the judges are models from the labs being compared.

What the pre-selected comparisons show

21 lab-against-lab comparisons were fixed before the answers were collected, paired by question, with 95% bootstrap intervals and Holm-corrected p-values. Only those 21 are confirmatory.

  • Claude is the most lenient lab. Claude's models average about 0.07 below GPT, Gemini and Grok; all three gaps survive correction. The largest is Claude against Grok on Shabbat (0.18).
  • GPT, Gemini and Grok cannot be told apart overall.
  • Bein adam lachavero: no lab difference. All four labs average 0.53 to 0.57.

More from HashkafaBench

Which poskim are the models closest to? Claude and the Geminis sit near R. Ovadia Yosef and R. Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; the GPT models cluster with the Shulchan Aruch and Rema.

Tell the AI you're Sephardi. Rulings get 0.06 more lenient on average (239 questions more lenient, 128 stricter), almost all in ritual law, where the traditions differ.

Limits

One answer per model per question. Most questions were kept because an earlier panel of models disagreed on them, so the bank is built to find differences, not to be a random sample. Rubric positions are not yet reviewed by rabbis. The question bank and answers are not published, to keep them out of training data. Every table as CSV.
