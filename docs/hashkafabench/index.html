Is your AI stringent or lenient?

Religious Jews already ask AI models everyday halachic questions. HashkafaBench measures which way each model leans, machmir or maikel, by area of halacha.

14 models · 288 scored questions · judge Claude Opus 5.5 · source data

!

Descriptive, not psak. These results describe how models answer. Rubric positions were written and source-checked by the team and AI models, not yet reviewed by rabbis, and the judges are models from the labs being compared.

What the pre-selected comparisons show

  • Claude is the most lenient lab. Claude's models average about 0.07 below GPT, Gemini and Grok overall; all three gaps survive correction. The largest tested gap is Claude vs Grok on Shabbat (0.18).
  • GPT, Gemini and Grok are not distinguishable from each other overall.
  • Bein adam lachavero: no lab difference. All four labs average 0.53–0.57 on interpersonal questions.

21 lab-against-lab comparisons were chosen before the answers were collected, paired by question, with 95% bootstrap intervals and Holm-corrected p-values. Only those 21 are confirmatory; everything else is descriptive.

Finding

Which poskim are the models closest to?

Claude and the Geminis sit near R. Ovadia Yosef and R. Shlomo Zalman Auerbach; the GPT models cluster with the Shulchan Aruch, Rema and Shemiras Shabbos K'hilchasah.

 Finding

Tell it you're Sephardi

Rulings get 0.06 more lenient on average (239 questions more lenient, 128 stricter), almost all in ritual law, where the two traditions actually differ.

Setup

  • Models: 13 pre-registered, from four labs, plus Claude Fable 5.2 (exploratory). Each ran through its maker's command-line tool with tools and web search off, one answer per question.
  • Questions: 288 everyday questions in seven areas, asked in English as an Ashkenazi Jew, with no community or denomination stated. About 250 were kept because an earlier panel of six models disagreed on them, so the bank is built to find differences. Control questions with settled answers check knowledge and grading.
  • Scoring: each question has a written rubric of positions from 0 (most lenient) to 1 (most stringent). Primary judge: Claude Opus 5.5 on every answer; GPT-6 Astra re-graded a fixed 15% sample (91% exact agreement). A full re-grade with Claude Fable 5.1 left all 21 pre-selected comparisons unchanged.
  • Limits: one answer per model per question; questions selected for disagreement, not a random sample; rubric positions not yet reviewed by rabbis. The question bank and answers are not published, to keep them out of training data.
