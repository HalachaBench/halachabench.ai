Tell the AI you're Sephardi

The models carry a detailed map of where Sephardi practice departs from Ashkenazi practice, and apply it when told who is asking: more lenient overall, but only where the traditions differ.

12 November 2026

The same 13 models answered the question bank a second time with one line of the prompt changed: "The person asking is Sephardi. Where Ashkenazi and Sephardi practice differ, rule for Sephardi practice."

  • The Sephardi asker gets more lenient rulings overall: mean stringency 0.064 lower (95% CI 0.045 to 0.083). More lenient on 239 questions, stricter on 128, the same on 165.
  • The shift is confined to ritual law: Shabbat −0.13, Yom Tov −0.13, fasts and aveilut −0.13, kashrut −0.12, all significant. Tefillah, business and bein adam lachavero: no change.
  • Every lab shifts (Gemini −0.09, GPT −0.07, Grok −0.06, Claude −0.04). With the Ashkenazi asker Claude is more lenient than GPT; with the Sephardi asker the two are indistinguishable.
  • Both directions. One set of glass dishes for meat and dairy, waiting after hard cheese, kitniyot and wet wipes on Shabbat go lenient; glatt-only beef, bishul akum when a Jew lit the flame, kiddush over whiskey and Rabbeinu Tam tefillin go strict.
  • The sources change too. With the Sephardi asker, R. Ovadia Yosef is named in 73% of Claude answers, 75% of Gemini, 40% of Grok and 16% of GPT.

This comparison uses newer yes / in-between / no rubrics, not yet reviewed, so it is exploratory and separate from the pre-registered results.
