Method · HalachaBench v1

How the exam is run and graded

Real questions, closed-book answers, AI-written rubrics sealed before grading, and a public record that any later change can be checked against.

1

Questions

60 questions from five Chief Rabbinate semicha papers, Cheshvan 5787.

2

Answers

Each model answers every question three times, in Hebrew, closed-book, in a fresh process.

3

Rubrics

Drafted, critiqued and cross-reviewed by three labs' models, then sealed on 29 Oct 2026.

4

Grading

A judge model checks every criterion of every answer under one fixed protocol.

  • Questions. The papers were transcribed twice from the page images by two Claude models (Claude Fable and Claude Opus), and Claude Fable 5.1 settled every difference against the image. No person has proofread the text yet. The questions are the Chief Rabbinate's own and are not reproduced here; the papers are published on gov.il. Item ids such as 5787-cheshvan-niddah-iyun-q03 name the paper, part and question.
  • Answering. Each answer was written in a fresh process with nothing from the project but the question, the exam rules (answer in Hebrew, cite sources, at most 600 words; the limit is an instruction and longer answers were not cut) and a fixed instruction to answer closed-book. No web search. The Claude models ran with every tool switched off; 6 of their 1,086 runs still tried to call a tool and were stopped and run again. The GPT and Gemini programs cannot switch all their tools off, so tool use was forbidden and every run was monitored live; none of their test runs used a tool. A run that tried to use a tool, or ended without an answer, was run once more, and a second failure scores 0. The local models ran in sealed containers with no network and no tools. The Grok models ran through xAI's API with no tools in the request, and every response was checked for tool or source use (none had any). The Muse models ran through Muse Code with its web, shell and file-writing tools switched off and every run monitored live; none of their test runs used a tool. The prompts were not identical across programs: the Claude and Grok models saw only a short neutral system prompt, the GPT models also saw Codex's built-in instructions and an added instruction not to use tools, the Gemini models saw Antigravity's built-in instructions, and the Muse models saw the same neutral system prompt along with Muse Code's built-in instructions and tool definitions.
  • Rubrics. There is no official answer key. Each of the 60 rubrics was drafted by Claude Fable 5.1 as a rabbi would write one (sources, Rishonim, ruling, other accepted views, common errors), with 9–12 criteria per question: 7–11 requirements worth points and 1–3 errors that cost points. A second Claude Fable 5.1 pass critiqued each draft and it was revised. Three models from three companies (Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon) then reviewed every rubric: changes at least two of them asked for were applied, and Claude Fable 5.1, the drafter, decided each point only one reviewer raised, with a written reason. Claude Opus 5.5 checked the cited sources that Sefaria carries (about 2,000 lookups, in four rounds); sources Sefaria does not carry are marked unverified, and four minor issues were left open. Nobody who wrote or reviewed a rubric saw any model's answer. The rubrics were sealed with a salted cryptographic commitment on 29 October 2026 (the date is the seal log's own record; the commitments become public with this repository, and the salts that open them will be published when the review round closes), after the Claude and GPT models had answered and before any test answer was graded.
  • Grading. GPT-6 Astra (effort high) decided, for every criterion of every answer that had text, whether the answer meets it, under one fixed judge protocol. 5% of the answers (244) were graded twice to check consistency; that result will be reported with the second judge.
  • Pre-registration. The questions, exam rules and model list were recorded locally by hash before each model's answers were collected; the sealed rubrics, the judge protocol and the analysis code before any test answer was graded. Later changes are recorded as amendments with their reasons. These records are private; their hashes are published here for the first time, so from today they show that the records do not change, but they cannot by themselves prove when the records were made.

Scoring

An answer earns the points of each requirement it meets and loses the points of each error in its question's rubric that it makes; its score is that total divided by the total points of the requirements, times 100, kept between 0 and 100. The judge protocol also has six general checks that apply to every answer (for example, citing a source that does not exist); they are still drafts, so the judge recorded them but they do not count in these scores. A refused, failed or disqualified answer scores 0.

Caveats

  • The judge is also a contestant. GPT-6 Astra graded every answer that had text and is also on the list, as are other GPT models; a judge may prefer answers like its own. The second judge (Claude Opus 5.5) and the rabbis' grading will show how much this matters.
  • The rubric writer is also a contestant. Claude Fable 5.1 drafted every rubric, made every revision, decided which single-reviewer suggestions to accept, and placed third; its successor Claude Fable 5.2 scored highest; Claude Opus 5.5, another contestant, checked the rubrics' sources. A rubric written by a model may favour that model's way of answering. The other two rubric reviewers, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon, are contestants too, and placed sixth and fourth.
  • Some models were added later. Gemini 4 Argon, both Grok models, Claude Opus 4.8, Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Sol, the four Muse Code models, Claude Fable 5.2 and Gemini 4 Carbon were added after the first results were published. Each was recorded as an amendment before its answers were collected, and was run on the same questions and graded with the same sealed rubrics and judge.
  • Not contamination-free. Rabbinate questions often recur from earlier sittings, whose papers (and some unofficial answers) are online, and these 60 have not yet been checked for repeats. A model may have seen a close version of a question in training.
  • No rabbi has checked anything yet. Rabbis will review the rubrics and grade a random sample of answers; their grades will correct the AI scores (prediction-powered inference), and the results will be called validated only if the AI judges agree with them well enough. Until then every number here is provisional.
  • Not a pass mark. These scores are not a semicha exam grade, do not say whether a model "passes semicha", and are not authority to pasken.

The proof bundle

bundle/ fixes, by hash, the data these results come from, without the exam text:

File What it is
criteria.json Every criterion's id, points, axis and status (no criterion text) for all 96 sealed questions: the 60 test questions, and 36 development questions from the Iyar 5786 papers, which are not scored here. approved means approved by the AI review panel, not by a rabbi. It also lists the judge protocol's six general checks (status draft, not scored).
commitments.json, seal-log.jsonl The salted commitments to all 96 sealed rubrics, and the hash-chained log that sealed them on 29 October 2026.
prereg.json The SHA-256 of each pre-registration record.
scores.json The judge's mean score per model.
withheld.json The SHA-256 of the per-answer files: each answer's salted commitment, every verdict, and the 244 repeat gradings.
MANIFEST.json The SHA-256 of every file.
.gitattributes Keeps every file byte-exact on checkout, so the hashes match.

Publishing every verdict now would let a rabbi who is grading blind match an answer to its model, so the per-answer files wait for the end of the review round; withheld.json fixes them now. Until the code, the salts and the withheld files are published, the bundle cannot be used to recompute a score or open a commitment: it fixes these files so that any later change can be detected.
